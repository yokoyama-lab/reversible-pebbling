## Important novelty correction: truncate Lee Resolve
Source https://arxiv.org/html/2610.09434v1 §§3.4,5.3, in particular Lemmas3.6/3.7, eqs14,17,19,20. Their algorithms already imply a family of stronger linear-work upper bounds than our single clean-fringe baseline. The construction below is a consequence of PRIOR techniques, not a new algorithmic idea.
Let H be complete-tree height, n=2^H-1 and s=Theta(n/H), reserving one output pebble and using b=s-1 below it. Constants in Theta(s) are fixed. For sufficiently large H, b>=2H.
1. Run their ParallelPebble(H,s), retaining output: work n, depthO(H+n/s). It leaves internal ghosts but no lower pebbles.
2. On each of the two child subtrees of height H-1 choose k with2^k the largest power of2<=b/4. It satisfies both cut conditions: (H-1)+2^k-k<=b and2^(k+1)-1<=b. Apply their Lemma3.6 to each child. WorkO(n), depthO(H+n/s), no pebbles below output. Remaining forest height r0=(H-1)-k=log2(n/s)+O(1)=O(log H), and conserved mass M=A*2^r=n+1. Actual vertex mass is A*(2^r-1)<=M.
3. For a stage with r>16 use exactly their cut q=ceil(log2 r)+2 and local budget p(r)=min(2^r-1,ceil(16*2^r/r)). At most floor(b/p(r)) jobs concurrently. Monotonicity and b>=p(r0) ensure at least one job fits; this latter condition holds at s~n/H for sufficiently large H because2^r0=Theta(H). Their explicit proof gives per-stage work at most C_stage*M and depth at most C_depth*(r+M/b), with absolute constants (not numerically extracted). New forest height is q and mass remains M. No simultaneous-interference issue is introduced: jobs are disjoint and batches separated, output pebble budget reserved.
4. After j such stages (or earlier r<=16), STOP recursive Resolve. Fully pebble then unpebble each remaining ghost-containing tree using its m=2^r-1 registers, batching floor(b/m) trees. This is feasible because m<=2^r0=Theta(H)<<b. It clears arbitrary ghosts too. Work<=2M; depth<=2r*ceil(A/floor(b/m))=O(r+Mr/b). This terminal step is already the base-case mechanism described in Lemma3.7, applied earlier when jobs fit.
Hence W<=(C_start+C_stage*j+2)*O(n) and D=O(H+j*n/s+(n/s)*r_j), with r_{i+1}=ceil(log2 r_i)+2 until bounded. The constants can be chosen uniform in j over the actual stages because each uses the same lemma. Sum r_i=O(r0) since r_{i+1}<=r_i/2 for r_i>16. At s~n/H: D=O(H*(1+j+r_j)).
For EACH FIXED j, r_j is an iterated logarithm of H (r0~log H), so the upper bound is O(H*log^{(j+1)} H) with W=O_j(n). In particular j0 reproduces the O(H log H) clean-fringe order; j1 improves to O(H loglog H), etc. This absorbs the elementary baseline into prior block/forest methods. Do not market O(HlogH) as new.
If j grows with H, the work factor j grows too. At j~log*H one recovers their W=O(n log*H) and D=O(H log*H) endpoint, NOT a linear-work result with absolute constant. Quantifiers matter: for fixed prescribed cap W<=C*n, only stage counts whose ACTUAL constants fit C may be used. Published O constants do not specify those counts. No fixed-C optimal curve follows automatically.
